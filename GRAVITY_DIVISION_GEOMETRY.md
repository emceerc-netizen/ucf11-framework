The 4 anchor dimensions represent the compact binding core of the mass configuration. This is the lowest-order geometric source of attraction, the rigid center of the field.

In practical terms, this is the region where:
- the central attractor is strongest
- the local field is most tightly bound

This is associated with the strongest, most compact gravitational response in the model.

### 2.2 12 Transition Dimensions

The 12 transition dimensions represent the coupling layer between compact core geometry and the macroscopic shell. This is the intermediate region where the system redistributes force and reorients the field structure as the geometry projects into lower dimensions.

This layer is responsible for:
- rotational coupling
- geometric mediation between dense core and diffuse envelope
- the separation between compact and shell-dominated response modes

This is the layer most naturally associated with the `36` rotational plane residual arising from the combinatorial topology.

### 2.3 8 Projected Shell Dimensions

The 8 projected shell dimensions correspond to the outermost observable layer, the part that becomes the effective 3D field we experience as the observed universe.

This is the regime most relevant to:
- diffuse galaxies
- ultra-diffuse systems
- weakly-bound outer envelopes
- shell-dominated acceleration profiles

In this layer, geometric response is less compact and more dependent on the smooth radial distribution of mass.

## 4. Why the Geometry Splits Galaxy Regimes

The observed model behavior is not random. It falls naturally into three classes:

### Spiral galaxies

Spiral systems are more coherent and more strongly tied to the compact binding layer. Their dynamics respond more strongly to the inner geometric coupling, which is why the nested field response is favored:

```text
a_E = sqrt(a_N^2 + k_factor * a_buoyant * a_N)
```

This indicates that the core + transition layers dominate the response.

### Ultra-diffuse / dwarf systems

Diffuse systems are more shell-dominated. Their response is better captured by the envelope-based law:

```text
a_E = sqrt(a_N * a_buoyant)
```

with the distributed radial profile carrying most of the effect.

This indicates that the shell geometry dominates the response in low-density regimes.

### Elliptical systems

Ellipticals sit near the compact-end response but with a more extended mass envelope, which is why the distributed-envelope model often produces the best fit without needing the strongest nested coupling.
